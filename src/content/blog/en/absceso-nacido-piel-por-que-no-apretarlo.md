# Skin Abscess or Boil: Why Not to Squeeze It

Many Spanish-speaking families call it a «nacido», and when it shows up in the armpit some say «golondrino». It starts as a small bump that stings when your clothes rub against it, and within a couple of days it turns into a red, hot, tight lump that throbs with every step. Nearly everyone feels the same urge: press it with their fingers, poke it with a needle heated on the stove, or cover it with whatever a neighbor swears by, hoping it will "pop on its own."

This article explains what that lump really is, why squeezing it usually ends worse than people expect, and at what point it makes sense to have the clinic's medical team open it with numbing medicine and sterile instruments.

## What is going on under the skin

An abscess is a pocket of pus the body builds to wall off an infection. It often begins in a follicle, the tiny opening a hair grows from, which is why it tends to appear where there is friction, sweat or hair: the armpits, groin, buttocks, back and face. Here is how the clinic's [abscess drainage](/en/services/drenaje-abscesos) page puts it: a lump that is red, warm to the touch, swollen and sore, soft or watery in the middle, at times showing a white or yellow head, and now and then bringing a fever.

That "full pocket" feeling is the key. While the pus is trapped, pressure builds and so does the pain. Relief comes once the pressure is released, but how and where it is released matters a great deal.

## Why squeezing it at home backfires

When you press a boil with your fingers, the force doesn't push all the pus out. Some of it is driven inward, into deeper tissue and, at worst, into the bloodstream. That is exactly what the clinic warns about on its service page: squeezing or piercing an abscess usually makes it worse.

Other problems pile on top:

- **The pocket never fully empties.** A needle hole lets out a little pus, the skin seals over with infection still inside, and the lump comes back.
- **Fingers and needles are not sterile.** New germs get added to an area that is already infected.
- **Pressing and rubbing inflame the skin around it.** The redness can spread into a wider skin infection.
- **Home remedies only cover the problem.** Thick ointments, leaves, garlic or a hot tomato slice do not draw the pus out, and they may irritate the skin or delay a visit.

## What you can safely do while you decide

As general health guidance, while the bump is still small:

- Place a clean, warm compress over it several times a day; the warmth helps the body bring the infection toward the surface.
- Wash your hands before and after touching it, and cover it with clean gauze if it oozes.
- Don't share towels, razors or clothing with the rest of the household while the lump is there.
- Wear loose clothing if it sits in the groin, buttocks or armpit, so it isn't rubbed all day long.

If the lump keeps growing, hurts more or you develop a fever despite these steps, waiting at home is no longer the right call.

## When to have it drained at the clinic

This table sums up the most common situations and what to do in each:

| What you notice | What to do |
|---|---|
| Small, firm red bump, no fever | Warm compresses at home and keep an eye on it |
| Soft or fluid-filled lump with a white or yellow tip | Clinic visit to decide on drainage |
| The lump is growing or the pain keeps you from sleeping or sitting | Clinic visit |
| You have diabetes, a weak immune system or several boils at once | Clinic visit, without putting it off |
| Boil near the eyes or nose, on the neck, or in the genital or anal area with severe pain | Emergency room |
| High fever with chills or redness spreading fast | ER or 911 |

When it isn't clear whether the lump already holds pus, the medical team can check it with an [in-clinic ultrasound](/en/services/ultrasonido) before deciding whether to open it.

## Warning signs: skip the clinic and call 911

A boil can turn serious. With any of these changes a clinic visit is no longer enough, and the right move is the ER or a call to 911:

- Red skin spreading quickly, together with fever.
- Red streaks running from the lump toward the groin or armpit.
- Confusion, unusual drowsiness or feeling about to pass out.
- Sudden puffiness in the lips, tongue or whole face, or struggling to pull in air.
- A wound that keeps bleeding even under firm pressure.

## What happens when the boil is drained at the clinic

At Clínica Hispana Familiar 529 skin abscesses are drained under local anesthesia during the same visit. The procedure goes like this:

1. The medical team examines the lump and the skin around it.
2. The skin over the boil is disinfected and its whole border is numbed with local anesthesia.
3. A small incision lets all of the pus out, not just part of it.
4. The cavity is rinsed with sterile solution and, if the abscess is large, gauze is left inside so it keeps draining.
5. It is covered with a bandage, and home care is explained.

What surprises people most is the relief: according to the service page, pain drops almost right away once the pressure is released. The wound is not stitched shut, because it has to heal from the inside out.

## Do you always need an antibiotic?

Not always. The main treatment for an abscess is getting the pus out. The clinic prescribes an antibiotic when the skin around it is infected too (cellulitis), when there is fever, when the abscess is larger than 5 centimeters, when there are several, or when the patient has diabetes or a weak immune system. If one is needed, the prescription is sent electronically to the [pharmacy](/en/services/farmacia) you choose, and it is worth finishing the whole course even after the lump stops hurting.

## The days after drainage

At home, change the bandage as instructed and keep up the warm compresses, which help the wound go on draining. A few days later there is a follow-up visit to remove or change the inner gauze, and any dressing changes after that are handled through the [wound care](/en/services/curacion-heridas) service. If during those days you notice foul-smelling pus, more redness, fever or growing pain, come back to the clinic.

## When the boil keeps coming back in the same spot

Some people get one boil after another. It is worth mentioning at the visit, for two reasons:

- **Uncontrolled high blood sugar makes skin infections more likely.** If your glucose hasn't been checked in a long time, the medical team may order a blood test to clear things up.
- **Sometimes it isn't a one-off abscess but a cyst that gets infected again and again.** The [minor surgery](/en/services/cirugias-menores) page lists removal of cysts that become inflamed, hurt or get infected repeatedly.

## Before you come in: three details that help

To make the visit for your boil go faster, keep in mind:

- how long you have had the lump and whether you already squeezed it, poked it or put something on it;
- whether you have had fever or chills, and whether you have diabetes;
- whether anyone else at home has had similar lumps.
